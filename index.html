The paper

Making diverse bodies explicit evaluation targets

Human pose and mesh benchmarks largely assume limb-complete bodies. LDBody supports evaluation of residual anatomy, prosthesis-supported motion, and variation in body proportions through two complementary components: LDBody Images and LDBody Motion. Together, they cover limb differences, dwarfism, and multiple prosthesis types.

The annotation framework distinguishes body joints, residual-limb endpoints, and prosthesis-supported landmarks under a protocol guided by internationally accredited Para Athletics classifiers. The image component adds prosthesis annotations to 8,866 LDPose images while retaining existing body-joint and residual-limb labels. The motion component combines eight synchronized RGB views with Vicon-derived keypoints and Vicon-constrained fitted body meshes.

Benchmarks cover 2D pose, multi-view 3D pose, residual-limb localization, and human mesh recovery. The evaluated cases reveal persistent errors at residual endpoints and affected distal joints. Similar joint errors can also coexist with substantially different fitted-surface agreement, motivating separate evaluation of these targets.

LDBody Motion

Real movements, synchronized views

136 synchronized tests from five adult male participants with lower-limb terminal deficiency form the reported motion benchmark: three with transtibial and two with transfemoral deficiency.

  • 1,088 view-specific videos and 963,104 RGB frames.
  • 120 prosthesis-worn and 16 non-worn tests.
  • Static/postural, gait/locomotion, transitional/functional, and running/sport dynamic activities.
  • Vicon-derived 2D/3D keypoints and fitted mesh references.

Expert-guided annotations

Three distinct landmark types

Shared definitions preserve the distinction between residual anatomy and prosthesis-supported function.

01 · Body joints

Standard body landmarks

Body joints follow standard pose layouts. Motion exports provide OpenPose-style 25-body-slot and COCO-style 17-body-slot layouts, each extended with eight residual-limb slots.

02 · Residual anatomy

Residual-limb endpoints

Endpoints identify existing body structure and are evaluated separately. In non-worn cases, unavailable distal keypoints remain unlabeled.

03 · Supported function

Prosthesis landmarks

Visible, functionally relevant standard-layout landmarks are added on worn prostheses while retaining residual endpoints. These are functional targets rather than anatomical joints.

Vicon-constrained fitting

Anatomical and kinematic mesh references

Two fitted-reference branches support separate assessment of residual anatomy and prosthesis-supported motion.

Anatomical reference

Represents residual-limb body structure. Fitting uses Vicon-derived body keypoints and valid residual-endpoint constraints. Evaluation uses anatomically present joints and residual-topology vertices.

Kinematic reference

Retains a standard full-body layout for prosthesis-supported motion. Fitting uses functional keypoints, including available prosthesis-supported landmarks.

Vicon-Constrained Mesh Reference Generation

LDBody’s meshes are measurement-constrained fitted references, not unrefined HMR predictions.

Constraining body shape as well as pose.

In the marker-constrained fitting demonstrated on this page, image-based HMR provides the initialization. Vicon-derived joint centres and valid surface-marker trajectories then constrain body pose, global orientation, translation, and the body-shape parameters β. Marker-to-surface correspondences are represented by mesh triangles and barycentric coordinates and remain fixed after initial calibration. Minimizing the discrepancy between these model-attached points and the measured markers supplies a data constraint on the body surface and β, rather than leaving body shape determined solely by the image initializer. A single β is optimized and shared within each sequence, with temporal and initialization regularization stabilizing the fit.

An established approach to reference construction.

AMASS uses MoSh++ to recover parametric human bodies from optical motion-capture markers and evaluates surface reconstruction against synchronized 4D scans [1]. MOYO explicitly uses MoSh++-recovered SMPL-X parameters as ground truth [2]. These works provide a precedent for using motion-capture-fitted bodies as benchmark references. LDBody does not directly use MoSh++; our pipeline shares the principle of fitting a body model to measured marker evidence, while using image-based initialization, explicit joint constraints, and residual-limb-specific processing. The cited studies support this reference-construction approach, not an equivalent accuracy guarantee for our implementation.

Separate reference targets.

The kinematic branch retains a standard body layout for prosthesis-supported motion, rather than reconstructing the device surface. The anatomical branch represents the remaining body and residual limb. Residual-endpoint constraints apply only to the anatomical branch. The local vertex refinements used in the demonstrations are distinguished from the underlying parameterized fits.

Checks reported in the submitted paper.

Appendix D reports fitting residuals, shared-region discrepancies between branches, and reference sensitivity. In the fixed-prediction reference-swap experiment, the tested full-body methods retain their MVE ordering under HSMR- and PromptHMR-initialized references, although absolute MVE values change. Joint and endpoint metrics use Vicon-derived targets, whereas MVE measures agreement with the corresponding fitted surface. Because ResiHMR also contributes to anatomical-reference construction, its residual-topology results are reported separately, rather than used to claim a cross-topology performance winner [3].

About these demonstrations

These qualitative clips show Vicon-assisted joint and foot alignment, residual-limb endpoint fitting, and gentle head correction. Vicon also supplies the displayed markers and skeleton, so the videos do not establish independent prediction accuracy. The full-body fit does not use the residual endpoint; an endpoint shown on that view is an overlay only.

Camera orientation and image scale remain fixed throughout each clip, and paired meshes use the same camera and scale. In AP004’s gait clip, the camera follows the measured pelvis in the horizontal plane while preserving vertical motion; mesh and measurement coordinates remain unchanged. Scale may differ between clips to accommodate movement extent, with no normalization of participant height. In AP002, full-body calf shortening is distributed smoothly while preserving transverse shape and the fitted ankle and foot.

These are model-based fitted references, not anatomical surface scans. Mean vertex error measures agreement with the corresponding fitted surface. Appendix D reports reference-construction and sensitivity analyses.

Joint and surface errors differ

TokenHMR and PromptHMR have similar joint errors, while their mean vertex errors are 177.81 mm and 107.44 mm under the kinematic protocol.
